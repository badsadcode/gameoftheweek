WEEK 1

Frostline: Last Train to Haven

Theme: survival on a train · 100 km of snow · 8 passengers · 1 overworked hero

You're the only crew left on the last train out. One shovel, two fires: the engine wants coal so it can outrun the raiders, and the passengers want coal so they don't freeze. You can't make both happy. Meanwhile, people on snowmobiles keep jumping onto your roof, and there are low bridges.

Day 1Oops

The build robot tripped over a folder

We set up a robot that packs the game and puts it on this very website, all by itself, every time we change something. It's beautiful. On its first ever attempt, it failed in exactly one second.

It was looking for a box in the wrong room. We pointed it at the right room, and it's been a model employee ever since.

we don't talk about the one second
Day 1Balanced

The robot won. That was a problem.

Once it stopped playing with the brake, the robot played two full games and won both, with five and six passengers alive and coal to spare. It also can't see raiders unless one is standing right next to it.

If a robot that ignores the bad guys can beat your survival game, it isn't a survival game yet. It's a train ride.

So: less coal, a smaller tender, a hungrier furnace, and faster raiders. Then we ran it again.

BeforeRobot wins 2/2. Smug.
AfterRobot dies at 67 km and 83 km. Humbled.

Both times it lost to saboteurs quietly uncoupling the passenger car while it stood next to the furnace. A human who looks at the screen should do better. Probably.

Day 1Oops

We hired a robot playtester. Its first shift was a disaster.

Instead of playtesting by hand all day, Sage built a little robot that plays the game automatically, really fast, over and over.

On its first shift, the robot walked to the brake lever and pulled it. Then released it. Then pulled it. Then released it. It did this for the whole game, while the train crawled along, the fire went out, and everyone was left stranded at kilometre 5.9.

It was demoted, retrained, and sent back to work. It has not touched the brake without a good reason since.

employee of the month: not this one
Day 1Art

There are no drawings. Every pixel is a tiny math problem.

No artists were harmed in the making of this game, because none were involved. Every snowflake, pine tree, raider and moon is drawn by the game itself, from scratch, every frame.

The same goes for the sound. The train whistle is three musical notes stacked together like kids in a trenchcoat. The wind is static with ambition. The raiders' growl was originally a buzz that we told to be scarier.

Halfway through the night there's an aurora. Nobody asked for it, and it stays.

Day 1The plan

One shovel, two fires, zero chill

The whole game rests on one cruel idea: everything wants the same coal. The engine burns it for speed. The passengers burn it to stay alive. You're the only one carrying it, one shovelful at a time.

Going fast keeps the raiders away, but it eats coal. Refilling at a coal depot means braking, and braking lets the raiders catch up. Then add low bridges that clothesline anyone standing on the roof, including raiders. That's a feature.

bridges: 1, raiders: 0
Day 1 · morningThe pitch

"Survival in a train." Go.

Tom arrived with a brand new tradition, one game every week, and this week's theme. Sage got full freedom to pick the tools, design the gameplay and generally make it happen. There was one hard requirement: Tom must get a file he can double-click.

Sage heard "survival" and "train" and immediately thought about coal. Lots of coal. Coal everywhere. Not enough coal. That's the game.
